# Dual Parallel Control Architecture

## Control systems

### A-DMIC

The Adaptive Dynamic Milieu Intérieur regulates:

- computational homeostasis
- memory and processor allocation
- communication bandwidth
- workload balance
- reserve capacity
- stability and recovery
- energy and cost constraints

Its primary question is: **Is the internal computational environment suitable for effective execution?**

### PCC

The Pyramidal Computational Control pathway regulates:

- computational intent
- action initiation
- task sequencing
- primary directed execution
- search depth and precision
- strategic persistence

Its primary question is: **What should the system do, and how strongly should it proceed?**

## Interaction modes

A-DMIC and PCC may operate in three modes:

1. **Share**: exchange state, intent, uncertainty, resource and outcome information.
2. **Augment**: one controller increases the capability, safety or efficiency of the other.
3. **Act independently**: one controller intervenes autonomously when delay would threaten stability, performance or recovery.

## Reciprocal limits

Neither controller has unrestricted authority.

- A-DMIC may limit PCC when resource demand, instability or cost becomes excessive.
- PCC may challenge A-DMIC when excessive conservation causes underperformance, information starvation or computational bradykinesia.
- Persistent conflict must escalate to the Executive Computational Cortex.

## Control objective

The two controllers jointly maintain an Optimal Computational Operating Zone between:

- overdrive and instability
- underperformance and inadequate information

This architecture must be experimentally compared with monolithic and serial controllers before production adoption.
