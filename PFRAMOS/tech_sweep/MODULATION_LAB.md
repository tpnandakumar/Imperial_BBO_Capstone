# Candidate Modulation Laboratory

## Principle

A weak candidate may contain a strong transferable mechanism. PFRAMOS should therefore assess both present quality and latent mouldability before rejection.

## Modulation methods

- Partial adoption: retain only the useful mechanism
- Decomposition: split a broad method into specialised subnodes
- Constraint: restrict an unstable method to a safe operating region
- Simplification: remove costly or fragile components
- Role reassignment: use the method for validation, routing or monitoring rather than optimisation
- Recombination: combine complementary parts from several candidates
- Parameter modulation: alter thresholds, activation rules or resource budgets
- Conduit modulation: use the method only within selected coherence pathways

## Mouldability dimensions

- core idea strength
- modularity
- decomposability
- responsiveness to constraints
- recombination potential
- role-reassignment potential
- repair cost
- residual risk

## Safeguards

A moulded candidate must be treated as a new derived candidate. It must retain the complete lineage of the original source, every transformation applied, rejected variants and measured validation results.

No candidate can bypass evidence, safety, coherence, robustness, compute or energy gates merely because it appears mouldable.

## Nodal density

Modulation should prefer improving, merging or specialising existing candidates before adding redundant nodes. This allows continued nodal expansion while controlling nodal congestion.
