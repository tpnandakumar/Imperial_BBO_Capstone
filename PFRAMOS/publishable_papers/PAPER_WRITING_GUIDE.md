# PFRAMOS Scientific Paper Writing Guide

## Purpose

This guide governs the preparation of all papers in the PFRAMOS series.

## Writing standard

All manuscripts must use:

- British English
- precise scientific language
- direct sentences
- restrained claims
- explicit distinction between measured results, inference and speculation
- consistent terminology
- no em dashes
- no en dashes
- no formulaic AI-style phrases
- no unsupported superlatives
- no invented or illustrative statistics presented as evidence

## Story structure

Each paper should tell a clear scientific story:

1. What problem exists?
2. Why do existing approaches not fully solve it?
3. What specific question does this paper ask?
4. What method was used?
5. What was found?
6. What do those findings mean?
7. What remains uncertain?
8. How does the paper advance the next chapter in the PFRAMOS series?

## Section guidance

### Title

The title should identify the method and scientific contribution without promotional language.

### Abstract

The abstract should contain:

- background
- objective
- methods
- results
- conclusion

The results sentence must contain measured findings only.

### Introduction

The introduction should move from broad problem to specific gap. It should end with the research question and contribution.

### Methodology

The methodology must allow reproduction. It should specify:

- data sources
- provenance and licences
- inclusion and exclusion rules
- data form
- preprocessing
- experimental design
- baselines
- model or system version
- hyperparameters
- seeds
- validation strategy
- protected test strategy
- statistical analysis
- compute and energy measurement

### Results

Results should follow the pre-specified outcome order. Report negative and inconclusive findings. Do not interpret results in this section beyond what is required for clarity.

### Discussion

The discussion should begin with the principal findings, compare them with prior evidence, explain plausible mechanisms, state practical implications and then address limitations.

### Conclusion

The conclusion should be narrower than the discussion. It should state only what the evidence supports.

## Claim discipline

Use these labels internally while drafting:

- `MEASURED`: directly supported by project data
- `DERIVED`: reproducibly calculated from project data
- `EXTERNAL`: supported by cited literature
- `INFERENCE`: reasoned interpretation from measured or external evidence
- `HYPOTHESIS`: proposition not yet confirmed

Every claim must fit one category.

## Style audit

Before internal review, every manuscript must pass:

- dash removal
- explicit AI-marker removal
- repetitive transition review
- unsupported emphasis review
- reference verification
- terminology consistency
- figure and table traceability
- plagiarism and duplicate-publication review

## Human responsibility

Automated systems may assist with structure, checking and drafting, but the author remains responsible for accuracy, originality, interpretation, citation integrity and final wording.
