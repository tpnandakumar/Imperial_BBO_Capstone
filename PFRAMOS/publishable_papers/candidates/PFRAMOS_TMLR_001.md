## Abstract

### Background
Sequential black-box optimisation must make decisions from sparse observations while balancing fit, uncertainty, robustness and computational cost. Existing optimisation methods may provide strong candidate generation but often do not expose a unified, auditable mechanism for regulating multiple competing optimisation nodes, temporary terminality, coherence and emergent route discovery.

### Objective
To evaluate whether PFRAMOS can improve the quality, robustness and auditability of sparse sequential optimisation while controlling compute and energy cost.
