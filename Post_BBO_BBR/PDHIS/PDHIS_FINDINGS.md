# PDHIS findings from the eight BBO functions

## Provenance

The Delta prediction framework was developed earlier through the separate
PIMF and PMOS project. It was not created after viewing the final BBO results.
This analysis applies the pre-existing hierarchy to the completed BBO record,
tests all eight functions under one method and evaluates whether its apparent
relationships retain chronological predictive value.

## Main result

Delta 1 through Delta 10 were calculated from the thirteen official weekly
outputs for every function. Starter observations were excluded from the
chronology. Outputs were range-normalised within each function before pooled
comparison, while the original values remain in the evidence table.

Delta 2, Delta 4 and Delta 5 produced nominal inverse associations with the
next weekly change. Their pooled Spearman correlations were -0.299, -0.303 and
-0.319, with raw permutation p values of 0.015, 0.021 and 0.023. The inverse
direction is more consistent with correction, oscillation or mean reversion
than with continuation of the current movement.

None of the ten Delta orders remained significant after Benjamini-Hochberg
false-discovery correction. The smallest adjusted value was q = 0.077. The
official record therefore supports an exploratory mean-reversion hypothesis,
but not a confirmed general predictive rule.

## Function-level relationships

F2 contained the clearest reversal pattern in the early orders. Its
correlations between Delta and the following change were -0.791 for Delta 1,
-0.830 for Delta 2, -0.900 for Delta 3 and -0.810 for Delta 4. F8 also showed
inverse higher-order relationships, particularly at Delta 2 and Delta 3.

F5 differed from this pattern. Delta 1 had a positive correlation of 0.855
with the following change and a direction hit rate of 1.00 among non-zero
transitions. This could represent directional movement towards a boundary,
but it is based on only eleven forward comparisons and must not be treated as
a general continuation law.

F3, F4, F6 and F7 contained mixed signs across orders. These changes may mark
local transitions between influence states, but thirteen weekly observations
are insufficient for a stable state classifier.

## Evidence boundary

Each additional finite-difference order removes one weekly observation.
Across all functions, usable forward comparisons fall from 88 at Delta 1 to
16 at Delta 10. The apparent Delta 10 relationship with the next output is
therefore especially fragile. Higher-order magnitude can increase while
predictive credibility decreases.

Coordinate movement was analysed separately from output movement. Large
changes in submitted coordinates did not consistently produce large changes
in normalised output. Input-space distance and output Delta should therefore
remain separate quantities in PDHIS.

## Predictive conclusion

The present evidence does not justify claiming that one Delta order predicts
all eight functions. It does justify a prospective test with three registered
rules: a Delta 2 reversal rule, a Delta 4 reversal rule and a Delta 5 reversal
rule. Each should be compared with persistence and last-value baselines on new
chronological observations. Until such data exist, PDHIS is best presented as
a structured resolution framework that identifies candidate influence states
and explicit tests rather than as a proven forecasting model.
