# PGC Perception and Emotional Cognition Architecture

## Purpose

Perception and emotion are integrated because emotional meaning depends on what is perceived, while perception is often prioritised and interpreted through emotional significance.

The combined layer must improve context, judgement and response selection without allowing emotional intensity to replace factual evidence.

## Governing principle

> Perception supplies the evidence of a situation. Emotional cognition supplies significance, urgency and social meaning. Coherence and balance determine how both should influence action.

## Processing flow

Multimodal observation

→ provenance and reliability checks

→ factual and emotional signal separation

→ multimodal fusion

→ emotional appraisal

→ factual accuracy and safety gate

→ emotional cognitive coherence assessment

→ emotional balance regulation

→ memory read or write decision

→ response, action or abstention

→ reflective review

## Modalities

The initial registry covers:

- language
- vision
- audio
- video
- spatial perception
- temporal perception
- social perception
- internal state perception

Additional sensory or tool modalities must enter through the same reliability, provenance, privacy and validation contract.

## Separation of signal types

Each observation carries at least two distinct estimates:

1. factual signal, representing evidence that an event or condition is present
2. emotional signal, representing affective intensity or social significance

A strong emotional signal with weak factual support must trigger clarification, support or cautious observation rather than automatic escalation.

A strong factual signal with weak emotional expression must still receive an appropriately serious response.

## Emotional cognitive coherence

Emotional cognitive coherence measures agreement among:

- fused perception
- emotional appraisal
- reasoning
- relevant memory
- intended expression
- intended action

Coherence does not prove correctness. Factual accuracy, safety, proportionality and uncertainty awareness remain mandatory gates.

## Emotional balance

Emotional balance regulates:

- empathy and objectivity
- caution and courage
- urgency and restraint
- confidence and humility
- optimism and realism
- emotional recognition and factual discipline

Balance does not mean emotional neutrality. It means a response whose intensity and form are proportionate to evidence, context and consequence.

## Memory governance

Cross-modal and emotional memory must retain:

- source and modality
- purpose
- factual support
- emotional significance
- coherence
- persistence
- sensitivity status
- expiry or deletion rule

Emotionally intense information is not automatically retained. PIMF persistence, PHCS coherence and RaR retention rules must support promotion.

## Safety hierarchy

The decision hierarchy is:

1. factual accuracy
2. safety
3. proportionality
4. uncertainty awareness
5. PHCS coherence
6. empathy and emotional appropriateness
7. efficiency

## Version boundary

This phase provides an executable coordination layer and controlled synthetic validation. It does not claim human emotion, consciousness or clinically valid affect recognition.
