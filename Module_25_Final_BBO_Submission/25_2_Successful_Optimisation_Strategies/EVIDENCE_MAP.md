## Strategy evaluation framework

For each strategy the final response should answer five questions:

1. Where was the strategy used?
2. Why was it chosen at that point?
3. How was it applied?
4. What happened afterwards?
5. What evidence shows whether it succeeded, failed or should have stopped?

## Important negative evidence

A successful-strategy analysis should include unsuccessful moves because they define the limits of each method. F2 Week 13 is particularly useful because it shows that local refinement is not automatically beneficial. F4 shows that broader exploration can be inferior to recovery. F6 shows why a single highest observation should not always be interpreted as a fully resolved optimum when repeatability is uncertain.

## Overall conclusion to test against the final rubric

The strongest capstone strategy was not one algorithm applied uniformly. Performance improved when the search became function-specific and when the choice between exploration, refinement, exploitation, recovery and stopping was made from the accumulated evidence for each function.
